During a poker hand, you'll have several actions available to you depending on the situation. This guide explains every action and when you can use each one.

## The Actions

### Fold
**Give up your hand and sit out until the next deal.**

When you fold, you discard your cards and forfeit any chips you've already put into the pot. You won't be able to win the current hand, but you also won't lose any more chips on it.

**When to fold:** When your cards are weak and you don't think you can win, or when the bet is too expensive relative to your chances of winning.

### Check
**Stay in the hand without betting any chips.**

Checking is only available when no one has made a bet in the current round. It's essentially saying "I'll pass for now." If everyone checks, the next community card is dealt.

**When to check:** When you have a decent hand but want to see what other players do before committing chips, or when you're in an early position and want to play cautiously.

### Call
**Match the current bet to stay in the hand.**

When another player has made a bet or raise, you can call by putting in the same amount. This keeps you in the hand without increasing the bet.

**When to call:** When you have a reasonable hand and the price is right to continue, but you don't want to escalate the betting.

### Bet
**Be the first to put chips into the pot in a betting round.**

Betting is available when no one else has bet yet in the current round. You choose an amount to wager, and all other players must then decide whether to fold, call your bet, or raise.

**When to bet:** When you have a strong hand and want to build the pot, or when you want to pressure other players into folding.

### Raise
**Increase the current bet that another player has made.**

A raise forces all remaining players to put in more chips if they want to stay in the hand. You must raise by at least the amount of the previous bet or raise.

**When to raise:** When you have a very strong hand and want to build a bigger pot, or when you want to force players with weaker hands to fold.

### All-In
**Push all of your remaining chips into the pot.**

Going all-in means betting every chip you have at the table. If you don't have enough chips to match a bet, you can still go all-in for everything you have — you'll be eligible to win a portion of the pot proportional to your contribution.

**When to go all-in:** When you're very confident in your hand, when you're short-stacked and need to make a stand, or as a bold bluff to pressure your opponents.

### Post Blind
**An automatic forced bet at the start of each hand.**

You don't choose to post a blind — it happens automatically when you're in the small blind or big blind position. The small blind is posted by the player to the left of the dealer, and the big blind (double the small blind) is posted by the next player.

## Action Summary Table

| Action | When Available | Chips Required |
|---|---|---|
| **Fold** | Anytime it's your turn | None — you give up |
| **Check** | When no bet has been made this round | None |
| **Call** | When someone has bet or raised | Match the current bet |
| **Bet** | When no one has bet this round | Your chosen amount |
| **Raise** | When someone has already bet | More than the current bet |
| **All-In** | Anytime it's your turn | All of your remaining chips |
| **Post Blind** | Automatic in blind positions | Small blind or big blind amount |

## The Betting Rounds

Each hand has up to four betting rounds, and your available actions change based on what other players have done:

### Preflop (Before Community Cards)
After receiving your hole cards, the player to the left of the big blind acts first. You can fold, call the big blind, or raise.

### Flop (After 3 Community Cards)
Three community cards are revealed. The first active player to the left of the dealer acts first. You can check (if no bet), bet, call, raise, or fold.

### Turn (After 4th Community Card)
A fourth community card is revealed. Same betting options as the flop.

### River (After 5th Community Card)
The fifth and final community card is revealed. This is the last chance to bet before the showdown.

### Showdown
If two or more players are still in after the river, they reveal their cards. The best hand wins the pot.

<Callout type="info">
A hand can end early at any betting round if all players but one fold. The last player standing wins the pot without having to show their cards.
</Callout>

## Tips for Beginners

- **Don't be afraid to fold** — Folding weak hands is the foundation of good poker strategy. You'll play many more hands by saving chips on bad ones
- **Pay attention to position** — Acting later in a round gives you more information about what other players are doing
- **Size your bets wisely** — Betting too small invites callers; betting too big risks too many chips
- **Watch for patterns** — Notice how other players bet. Do they always raise with strong hands? Do they bluff often? This information is valuable
