**Heads-up poker** is a one-on-one format where you play against a single opponent. It's the purest form of poker — just you against one other player, with no one else at the table.

## What Is Heads-Up?

In heads-up poker, only **2 players** sit at the table. Every hand is played directly against your opponent, creating an intense, fast-paced experience. Heads-up is available in all three game types on HajiPoker:

- **Texas Hold'em** heads-up
- **Omaha 4-Card** heads-up
- **Omaha 5-Card** heads-up

## How Heads-Up Differs from Regular Poker

Playing heads-up changes the dynamics significantly:

| Aspect | Regular Table (3–9 players) | Heads-Up (2 players) |
|---|---|---|
| **Pace** | Moderate | Very fast |
| **Hands per hour** | Fewer | Many more |
| **Hand selection** | Tighter (fold more) | Wider (play more hands) |
| **Blinds** | Rotate among many players | Alternate between the two players |
| **Strategy** | Range-based | Reads and adjustments matter more |
| **Position** | Varies each hand | You're always either the button or the blind |

### Blind Structure in Heads-Up
In heads-up play, the two positions alternate every hand:
- **The dealer (button)** posts the small blind and acts first before the flop, but acts last on all subsequent rounds
- **The other player** posts the big blind

## How to Play Heads-Up

### Option 1: Create a Heads-Up Table
1. Go to the **Lobby**
2. Click **Create Table**
3. Set the **player count to 2**
4. Choose your game type and blind levels
5. Optionally set a password to invite a specific friend
6. Click **Create** and wait for your opponent to join

### Option 2: Find a Heads-Up Table
1. Browse the **Lobby**
2. Filter by tables with **2 seats**
3. Look for a table with 1 player waiting
4. Click **Quick Join** to sit down

### Option 3: Use the Heads-Up Feature
HajiPoker also has a dedicated **Heads-Up** section where you can quickly find or create one-on-one matches.

## Tips for Heads-Up Play

Heads-up poker requires a different approach than a full table:

- **Play more hands** — With only one opponent to beat, many hands that you'd fold at a full table become playable in heads-up
- **Aggression wins** — Being passive in heads-up lets your opponent control the action. Bet and raise more often
- **Pay attention to patterns** — With only one opponent, you can pick up on their tendencies quickly. Do they fold to raises often? Do they bluff a lot? Adjust accordingly
- **Position matters even more** — Being on the button in heads-up is a significant advantage because you act last on every post-flop round
- **Manage your bankroll** — Heads-up can be swingy. Make sure you have enough chips to weather some losing streaks

<Callout type="info">
Heads-up is widely considered the most skill-intensive form of poker. It's how most professional poker feuds and challenges are settled!
</Callout>

## Who Should Play Heads-Up?

- **Friends who want a quick match** — No need to wait for a full table. Create a private heads-up table and challenge a friend
- **Players looking to improve** — Heads-up forces you to play more hands and make more decisions, which accelerates learning
- **Competitive players** — If you enjoy direct competition and reading opponents, heads-up is for you
- **Short on time** — A quick heads-up session gives you plenty of action in a short period

## Common Questions

**Q: Is heads-up available for all game types?**
Yes! You can play heads-up in Texas Hold'em, Omaha 4-Card, and Omaha 5-Card.

**Q: Is the rake different for heads-up tables?**
The same rake structure applies to all tables regardless of the number of players.

**Q: Can I play heads-up in tournaments?**
Sit & Go tournaments start with more players, but the final stage of any tournament ends as a heads-up match between the last two players.

**Q: How do I challenge a specific friend to heads-up?**
Create a private table with 2 seats, set a password, and share it with your friend. See [Creating a Private Table](/docs/playing-poker/creating-a-private-table).
