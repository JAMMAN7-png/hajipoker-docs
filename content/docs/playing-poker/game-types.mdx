HajiPoker offers three exciting poker game types, each with its own strategy and style. Whether you prefer the classic simplicity of Texas Hold'em or the action-packed complexity of Omaha, there's a game for you.

## Texas Hold'em

Texas Hold'em is the world's most popular poker game and the most played variant on HajiPoker.

### How It Works

1. Each player is dealt **2 private cards** (called "hole cards") that only they can see
2. Five community cards are dealt face-up on the table in stages (flop, turn, river)
3. Players make the best possible 5-card hand using any combination of their 2 hole cards and the 5 community cards
4. The player with the best hand — or the last player remaining after everyone else folds — wins the pot

### Why Play Texas Hold'em?

- **Easy to learn** — The rules are straightforward, making it perfect for beginners
- **Strategic depth** — Despite simple rules, the game offers incredible depth for experienced players
- **Most tables available** — You'll always find a Texas Hold'em game running at your preferred stakes

## Omaha 4-Card

Omaha 4-Card (often just called "Omaha") is the second most popular poker variant. It's similar to Texas Hold'em but with more hole cards, which means more action and bigger pots.

### How It Works

1. Each player is dealt **4 private cards** (hole cards)
2. Five community cards are dealt face-up on the table in stages
3. Players **must** use exactly 2 of their 4 hole cards combined with exactly 3 of the 5 community cards to make their best 5-card hand
4. The best hand wins the pot

### Why Play Omaha 4-Card?

- **More action** — Four hole cards create more hand possibilities and bigger pots
- **Strategic variety** — The requirement to use exactly 2 hole cards adds an extra layer of strategy
- **Exciting draws** — Players often have strong drawing hands, leading to dramatic turn and river cards

<Callout type="info">
In Omaha, you **must** use exactly 2 of your hole cards and exactly 3 community cards. This is different from Texas Hold'em, where you can use any combination. It's the most common mistake new Omaha players make!
</Callout>

## Omaha 5-Card

Omaha 5-Card takes the excitement of Omaha even further by giving each player one additional hole card.

### How It Works

1. Each player is dealt **5 private cards** (hole cards)
2. Five community cards are dealt face-up on the table in stages
3. Players **must** use exactly 2 of their 5 hole cards combined with exactly 3 community cards to make their best 5-card hand
4. The best hand wins the pot

### Why Play Omaha 5-Card?

- **Maximum action** — The extra hole card creates even more hand combinations and possibilities
- **Bigger pots** — More players tend to stay in hands, building larger pots
- **Challenge** — The added complexity makes it a favorite for experienced players seeking a mental challenge

## Comparison Chart

| Feature | Texas Hold'em | Omaha 4-Card | Omaha 5-Card |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hole cards dealt | 2 | 4 | 5 |
| Community cards | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| Cards used from hand | Any | Exactly 2 | Exactly 2 |
| Cards used from board | Any | Exactly 3 | Exactly 3 |
| Action level | Moderate | High | Very High |
| Best for | Beginners & pros | Intermediate+ | Experienced |

## Game Phases

All three game types follow the same phases during each hand:

1. **Waiting** — Players are seated, waiting for enough players to start
2. **Preflop** — Hole cards are dealt, first round of betting
3. **Flop** — Three community cards are revealed, second round of betting
4. **Turn** — A fourth community card is revealed, third round of betting
5. **River** — The fifth and final community card is revealed, last round of betting
6. **Showdown** — Remaining players reveal their cards, and the best hand wins

## Which Game Should I Play?

- **New to poker?** Start with **Texas Hold'em**. The rules are the simplest and there are plenty of resources to learn strategy
- **Looking for more action?** Try **Omaha 4-Card**. The extra hole cards make every hand more exciting
- **Experienced and want a challenge?** Dive into **Omaha 5-Card** for maximum complexity and action
