HajiPoker offers **Sit & Go tournaments** — fast-paced poker competitions where you compete against other players for a share of the prize pool. Unlike scheduled tournaments, Sit & Go tournaments start as soon as enough players have registered.

## What Is a Sit & Go Tournament?

A Sit & Go (often abbreviated as "SNG") is a single-table tournament that begins when the required number of players have signed up. Everyone starts with the same number of chips, and you play until one player has all the chips. The last players standing win a share of the prize pool.

## How It Works

1. **Register** — Find a Sit & Go tournament in the Tournaments section and pay the buy-in fee
2. **Wait for players** — The tournament starts once at least **2 players** have registered
3. **Everyone starts equal** — All players begin with **10,000 chips** regardless of the buy-in amount
4. **Blinds increase** — Every **5 minutes**, the blind levels go up, forcing more action as the tournament progresses
5. **Players are eliminated** — When you lose all your chips, you're out of the tournament
6. **Last players win** — The final remaining players split the prize pool based on their finishing position

<Callout type="info">
Unlike cash games where chips have a direct monetary value, tournament chips are used only for competition. Everyone starts equal, and the prize pool is distributed based on finishing position, not chip count.
</Callout>

## Starting Chips

Every player in a Sit & Go tournament starts with **10,000 chips**. This is the same for every player, regardless of the buy-in amount. Your goal is to accumulate chips by winning hands and ultimately be the last player standing.

## The Blind Schedule

Blinds increase every 5 minutes across 10 levels, creating increasing pressure as the tournament progresses:

| Level | Small Blind | Big Blind | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 | 20 | 5 minutes |
| 2 | 15 | 30 | 5 minutes |
| 3 | 25 | 50 | 5 minutes |
| 4 | 40 | 80 | 5 minutes |
| 5 | 60 | 120 | 5 minutes |
| 6 | 100 | 200 | 5 minutes |
| 7 | 150 | 300 | 5 minutes |
| 8 | 200 | 400 | 5 minutes |
| 9 | 300 | 600 | 5 minutes |
| 10 | 500 | 1,000 | Until tournament ends |

As the blinds increase, short-stacked players are forced to play more aggressively or risk being blinded out. This ensures that tournaments reach a conclusion in a reasonable time.

## Tournament Costs

Each tournament has a **buy-in** plus a **fee**:

- **Buy-in** — The amount that goes into the prize pool
- **Fee** — A small service charge for running the tournament

The total cost to enter is the buy-in plus the fee. Both amounts are clearly displayed before you register.

## Tournament Strategy Tips

### Early Stage (Levels 1–3)
- Blinds are small relative to your stack — play patiently
- Focus on premium hands and avoid risky moves
- Observe your opponents' tendencies for later use

### Middle Stage (Levels 4–6)
- Blinds start to put pressure on smaller stacks
- Begin picking up the aggression
- Look for opportunities to steal blinds

### Late Stage (Levels 7–10)
- Blinds are very large — short stacks must act fast
- Big stacks can apply pressure to smaller stacks
- Every decision matters significantly

<Callout type="warn">
Once you're eliminated from a tournament, you cannot re-enter or add more chips. Make every decision count!
</Callout>

## What Happens When a Player Is Eliminated?

When a player loses all their chips:

- They are immediately eliminated from the tournament
- Their finishing position is recorded
- If they finish in a paying position, their prize is automatically sent to their wallet
- The tournament continues with the remaining players
